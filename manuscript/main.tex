\begin{abstract}
Local Friendliness (LF) no-go theorems show that quantum predictions for extended Wigner's friend scenarios are incompatible with Local Agency combined with the \emph{Absoluteness of Observed Events}. A test requires a superobserver to reverse the friend's measurement, and any record that leaks into modes outside the superobserver's control degrades the violation. We quantify this for pure-dephasing leakage with branch coherence retention $f$ and leakage $\ell = \sqrt{1 - f^2}$. For one leaky laboratory the optimum of the Semi-Brukner functional is $2\sqrt{1 + f^2} = 2\sqrt{2 - \ell^2}$; we give a Sum-of-Squares certificate valid in any unital $C^*$-algebra and identify the bound with the trade-off between CHSH violation and an eavesdropper's guessing probability from device-independent randomness certification, so that an LF violation certifies the privacy of the friend's record. For two leaky laboratories and two-qubit systems, CHSH-type functionals obey $S \le 2\sqrt{1 + f_A^2 f_B^2}$, and the Brukner and genuine LF facets of the $(3,3,2,2)$ polytope retain an excess of at least $f^4/(1+f^2)$: violations decay quadratically in $f$ with one leaky laboratory and quartically with two. The quartic law holds in every dimension for the Brukner functional but fails for the others when a friend's measurement is coarse-grained. For circuit implementations, a noise budget combining leakage with gate and readout errors predicts, at the effective noise of a recent one-friend hardware run, a violation of the two-friend Brukner inequality and places the genuine LF facet at its threshold. Facet ranks are machine-checked in Lean~4. We frame the results by distinguishing the laboratory horizon, set by leakage, from the causal horizon in a covariant, record-relative assignment of truth values.
\end{abstract}
