\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:dsw_ceilings}

\subsection{Limits of principle and limits of hardware}

Any criterion for when a registration ceases to be reversible meets John Bell's objection to ``for all practical purposes'' (FAPP) arguments~\cite{bell1990}: if irreversibility only reflected the limits of the superobserver's control, the valuation of Sec.~\ref{sec:horizons} would not be objective. Ordinary gravitational radiation does not help, because in Minkowski spacetime a registration and its reversal can be run slowly enough to make the radiated quadrupole power, $\propto G \dddot{Q}^2 / c^5$, arbitrarily small. Danielson, Satishchandran, and Wald (DSW)~\cite{danielson2023,danielson2025} have shown that this adiabatic loophole closes in principle whenever the spacetime has a Killing horizon, which includes black-hole, Rindler, and cosmological de Sitter horizons. The static field difference between two displaced record configurations (a mass $m$ or a charge $q$ separated by a distance $d$) then radiates soft quanta through the horizon at a constant rate, and after a holding time $T$ the expected numbers of entangling soft gravitons and photons are~\cite{danielson2023}
\begin{equation}
    \langle N_{\mathrm{GR}} \rangle \sim \frac{G m^2 d^4 T}{\hbar R_H^5}, \qquad \langle N_{\mathrm{EM}} \rangle \sim \frac{q^2 d^2 T}{\hbar \varepsilon_0 R_H^3},
    \label{eq:dsw_flux}
\end{equation}
for a horizon of radius $R_H$, with coherence retention $f \le \exp(-\frac{1}{2}\langle N \rangle)$. Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsw_flux} grows linearly with $T$, slowing the reversal makes matters worse, and once $\langle N \rangle \gtrsim 1$ no superobserver inside the horizon can restore $f \to 1$.

This limit is one of principle only. At cosmological de Sitter scales ($R_H \sim 10^{26}\,\mathrm{m}$), Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsw_flux} gives decoherence times $T / \langle N \rangle$ exceeding $10^{100}\,\mathrm{s}$ for a $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ friend displaced by $1\,\mathrm{m}$ and exceeding $10^{70}\,\mathrm{s}$ for one elementary charge displaced by $1\,\mathrm{m}$, against a Hubble time of $4 \times 10^{17}\,\mathrm{s}$; DSW make the same observation for the electromagnetic case~\cite{danielson2023}, and Ref.~\cite{danielson2025} shows that horizon-induced decoherence admits an equivalent local description that suitable material bodies can mimic. The leakage that limits $f$ in any laboratory is the ordinary electromagnetic, phononic, and thermal kind that Bell's objection targets, together with the gate and readout errors budgeted in Sec.~\ref{sec:noise_budget}. A processor makes that leakage a controlled parameter: a friend qubit coupled weakly to a spectator mode realizes any $f \in [0, 1]$, so that the dependence on the product $f_A f_B$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:bilateral} can be measured directly.

\paragraph{Contrast with gravity-related collapse.}
The limit above is a prediction of standard quantum theory and general relativity. Gravity-related collapse models predict something very different. In the Di\'osi--Penrose proposal~\cite{diosi1989,penrose1996} a superposition of two mass distributions decays on a time $\tau_{\mathrm{DP}} \sim \hbar / E_G$, where $E_G$ is the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the two distributions. For a uniform sphere of mass $m$ and radius $R$ displaced by $d \ge 2R$, $E_G = G m^2 \,[\,6/(5R) - 1/d\,]$ up to a convention-dependent factor of two. For $m = 70\,\mathrm{kg}$, $R = 0.3\,\mathrm{m}$, and $d = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ this gives $\tau_{\mathrm{DP}} \sim 10^{-28}\,\mathrm{s}$, and resolving the mass distribution at the nuclear scale shortens it further. The two gravitational answers therefore differ by more than $120$ orders of magnitude: horizon-induced decoherence leaves a macroscopic friend reversible in principle for longer than $10^{100}\,\mathrm{s}$, whereas gravity-related collapse forbids the reversal almost at once. A Local Friendliness test with a friend of increasing mass would in principle decide between them~\cite{wiseman2023}, but nothing of the kind is within experimental reach, and the parameter-free version of the model is already excluded by an underground search for collapse-induced radiation~\cite{donadi2021}. On superconducting processors the direct effect of spontaneous-collapse models on qubit superpositions is negligible~\cite{vischi2022}, so the tests of Sec.~\ref{sec:noise_budget} do not probe them.

\subsection{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Two-Friend Tests on Hardware:} The noise budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:noise_budget} places a two-friend Brukner test within reach of processors that have run the one-friend test, and a Genuine LF violation at the threshold of the same effective noise. Do these estimates survive energy relaxation, crosstalk, and friends of more than one qubit, and can a tunable leak map the dependence on the product $f_A f_B$?
    \item \textbf{Upper Bounds for the Genuine LF Facet:} Theorem~1 is an exact certificate for the one-friend functional, whereas for $S_{\mathrm{GLF}}(f)$ we have the lower bound of Proposition~1 and numerical optima. A closed-form or Navascu\'{e}s--Pironio--Ac\'{i}n certificate for $S_{\max}^{\mathrm{GLF}}(f)$ is open, as is the condition on the friends' measurements under which the quartic law of the two-qubit analysis survives in higher dimension (Sec.~\ref{sec:polytope_bounds}).
    \item \textbf{Graded Valuations:} Definition~1 treats membership in $\mathcal{R}_O(\tau)$ as all-or-nothing. What redundancy or dissipation distinguishes a transient entangling gate from a dynamically stabilized record, and is there a consistent valuation for $0 < f < 1$?
    \item \textbf{Detector Models in Curved Spacetime:} Embedding Fewster--Verch detector couplings~\cite{fewster2020} in Rindler or Schwarzschild backgrounds would give $f(T, R_H)$ for the two-friend functionals from first principles and connect Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsw_flux} to the bounds of Sec.~\ref{sec:polytope_bounds}.
\end{enumerate}
