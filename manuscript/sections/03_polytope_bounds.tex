\section{Polytope Geometry and Noncommutative Leakage Bounds}
\label{sec:polytope_bounds}

\subsection{The $(3,3,2,2)$ Extended Wigner's Friend Scenario and Polarity Convention}

Consider the canonical bipartite $(3,3,2,2)$ EWFS~\cite{bong2020} featuring two spacelike-separated laboratories: Charlie resides inside Alice's laboratory, and Debbie resides inside Bob's laboratory. In each run, a source prepares a bipartite quantum state shared between system degrees of freedom $S_A$ and $S_B$. Charlie measures $S_A$ in a fixed basis yielding outcome $c \in \{-1, +1\}$, and Debbie (when present) measures $S_B$ in a fixed basis yielding $d \in \{-1, +1\}$. Superobservers Alice and Bob subsequently choose measurement settings $x, y \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ and record binary outcomes $A_x, B_y \in \{-1, +1\}$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For $x = 1$ (respectively $y = 1$), Alice (Bob) opens the laboratory and directly reads out the friend's record, setting $A_1 = c$ ($B_1 = d$).
    \item For $x \in \{2, 3\}$ ($y \in \{2, 3\}$), Alice (Bob) applies the time-reversed laboratory unitary $U_A^\dagger$ ($U_B^\dagger$) to the accessible laboratory degrees of freedom to uncompute the friend's registration before measuring target observable $N_x$ ($M_y$) on $S_A$ ($S_B$).
\end{itemize}

In any realistic physical implementation, Charlie's measurement interaction $U_A$ weakly entangles the outcome branches with inaccessible environmental or spectator modes $E_A$ outside the support of Alice's reversal operation $U_A^\dagger$. By Stinespring dilation, if Charlie measures orthogonal projections $P_+, P_-$ ($P_+ + P_- = \mathbf{1}$, $A_1 = P_+ - P_-$), the joint post-measurement branches attach normalized environmental states $|e_+\rangle, |e_-\rangle \in \mathcal{H}_{E_A}$. We define the \emph{branch coherence retention} $f_A$ and \emph{environmental record leakage} $\ell_A$ by
\begin{equation}
    f_A \equiv |\langle e_+ | e_- \rangle| \in [0, 1], \qquad \ell_A \equiv \sqrt{1 - f_A^2} \in [0, 1].
    \label{eq:polarity_convention}
\end{equation}
Notice the physical endpoints of Eq.~\eqref{eq:polarity_convention}: perfect isolation corresponds to $\ell_A = 0$ ($f_A = 1$, fully reversible friend record), whereas complete environmental orthogonalization corresponds to $\ell_A = 1$ ($f_A = 0$, irreversible classical leakage into $E_A$ outside Alice's record-reversal control). Symmetric bilateral setups satisfy $f_A = f_B = f$ and $\ell_A = \ell_B = \ell$. In interferometric language, $\ell_A$ and $f_A$ are the which-way distinguishability and the fringe visibility of $E_A$ regarded as a which-way marker, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:polarity_convention} is the pure-marker case of the duality relation $\ell_A^2 + f_A^2 \le 1$~\cite{englert1996}.

\subsection{Exact Facet Classification of the $(3,3,2,2)$ LF Polytope}

A $(3,3,2,2)$ behavior is specified by the 15-dimensional expectation vector
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \vec{p} = \bigl(&\langle A_1 \rangle, \langle A_2 \rangle, \langle A_3 \rangle, \langle B_1 \rangle, \langle B_2 \rangle, \langle B_3 \rangle, \\
    &\langle A_1 B_1 \rangle, \dots, \langle A_3 B_3 \rangle\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{15}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Under Local Hidden Variables (LHV), the Bell polytope $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{LHV}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{15}$ is the convex hull of $2^6 = 64$ deterministic extreme points $a_x, b_y \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $\langle A_x B_y \rangle = a_x b_y$. Under Two-Friend Local Friendliness (AOE applied to $c, d, A_x, B_y$ combined with Local Agency), there exists a joint distribution $P(a_x, b_y, c, d | x, y)$ satisfying no-signaling and $A_1 = c$ when $x = 1$, $B_1 = d$ when $y = 1$. Conditioning on deterministic friend outcomes $(c, d) \in \{-1, +1\}^2$ leaves an arbitrary non-signaling $(2,2,2,2)$ box on settings $\{2, 3\} \times \{2, 3\}$, whose non-local extreme points are the 8 Popescu--Rohrlich (PR) boxes ($\langle A_x \rangle = \langle B_y \rangle = 0$ for $x, y \in \{2, 3\}$ and $E_{22} E_{23} E_{32} E_{33} = -1$ with $E_{xy} \in \{-1, +1\}$). Consequently, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{LF}}$ is spanned by $64 + 4 \times 8 = 96$ vertices in $\mathbb{R}^{15}$~\cite{bong2020}.

\begin{align}

    S_{\mathrm{Bruk}} &= -\langle A_1 B_1 \rangle + \langle A_1 B_2 \rangle - \langle A_2 B_1 \rangle - \langle A_2 B_2 \rangle \le 2, \label{eq:ineq_bruk} \\
    S_{\mathrm{BnLF}} &= \langle A_2 B_2 \rangle - \langle A_2 B_3 \rangle - \langle A_3 B_2 \rangle - \langle A_3 B_3 \rangle \le 2, \label{eq:ineq_bnlf} \\
    S_{I_{3322}} &= -\langle A_1 \rangle + \langle A_2 \rangle + \langle B_1 \rangle - \langle B_2 \rangle + \langle A_1 B_1 \rangle \nonumber \\
    &\quad - \langle A_1 B_2 \rangle - \langle A_1 B_3 \rangle - \langle A_2 B_1 \rangle + \langle A_2 B_2 \rangle \nonumber \\
    &\quad - \langle A_2 B_3 \rangle - \langle A_3 B_1 \rangle - \langle A_3 B_2 \rangle \le 4, \label{eq:ineq_i3322} \\
    S_{\mathrm{GLF}} &= -\langle A_1 \rangle - \langle A_2 \rangle - \langle B_1 \rangle - \langle B_2 \rangle - \langle A_1 B_1 \rangle \nonumber \\
    &\quad - 2\langle A_1 B_2 \rangle - 2\langle A_2 B_1 \rangle + 2\langle A_2 B_2 \rangle - \langle A_2 B_3 \rangle \nonumber \\
    &\quad - \langle A_3 B_2 \rangle - \langle A_3 B_3 \rangle \le 6. \label{eq:ineq_glf}
\end{align}
Crucially, while $S_{\mathrm{GLF}} \le 6$ holds on all 64 LHV vertices, equality $S_{\mathrm{GLF}} = 6$ is attained on only 14 LHV vertices whose homogeneous span has rank $12 < 15$ (since every tight LHV vertex simultaneously satisfies $1 + a_2 + b_1 + a_2 b_1 = 0$, $1 + a_1 + b_2 + a_1 b_2 = 0$, and $a_2 b_2 - a_2 b_3 - a_3 b_2 - a_3 b_3 = 2$). Including the PR-box LF vertices raises the tight homogeneous rank to $15$, confirming from first principles that $S_{\mathrm{GLF}} \le 6$ is a genuine facet of $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{LF}}$ and \emph{not} a facet of $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{LHV}}$.

These five functionals do not exhaust the facets of $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{LF}}$. The complete enumeration of Ref.~\cite{bong2020} yields 932 facets in nine inequivalent classes: two genuine LF classes, two $I_{3322}$ classes, the Brukner and Semi-Brukner classes, and three positivity classes. Equations~\eqref{eq:ineq_glf} and~\eqref{eq:ineq_i3322} represent the first genuine LF class and the $I_{3322}$ class with marginals on inputs 1 and 2; the second genuine LF class and the $I_{3322}$ class with marginals on inputs 2 and 3 are not analyzed below.

\subsection{Dimension-Independent $C^*$-Algebra Bound for One-Friend Leakage}

To avoid restricting Sec.~\ref{sec:horizons}'s algebraic framework to two-qubit spaces, we first establish the exact Semi-Brukner bound in arbitrary unital $C^*$-algebras. Suppose $\mathfrak{A}$ is any unital $C^*$-algebra containing commuting Alice and Bob subalgebras ($[\mathfrak{A}_A, \mathfrak{A}_B] = \{0\}$). Let Charlie's binary observable be $A_1 = P_+ - P_- \in \mathfrak{A}_A$ ($A_1^* = A_1$, $A_1^2 = \mathbf{1}$). When Charlie's registration leaks environmental states with $\langle e_+ | e_- \rangle = f e^{i\alpha}$ ($f \in [0, 1]$), tracing over $E_A$ after Alice's reversal $U_A^\dagger$ maps any target involution $N_0 \in \mathfrak{A}_A$ ($N_0^* = N_0, N_0^2 = \mathbf{1}$) to $\Phi_f(N_0) = P_+ N_0 P_+ + P_- N_0 P_- + f(e^{i\alpha} P_+ N_0 P_- + e^{-i\alpha} P_- N_0 P_+)$. Absorbing the phase unitary $W_\alpha = P_+ + e^{i\alpha} P_-$ into $N \equiv W_\alpha^* N_0 W_\alpha$ ($N^* = N, N^2 = \mathbf{1}$) decomposes $N = N_+ + N_-$ into $A_1$-even and $A_1$-odd components
\begin{equation}
    N_+ = \frac{1}{2}(N + A_1 N A_1), \qquad N_- = \frac{1}{2}(N - A_1 N A_1),
    \label{eq:even_odd_decomp}
\end{equation}
so that Alice's effective Heisenberg observable for $x = 3$ takes the universal form $\tilde{A}_3(f) = N_+ + f N_-$.

\begin{theorem}[Noncommutative $C^*$-Algebraic Leakage Bound]
Let $\mathfrak{A}$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra, let $A_1, N \in \mathfrak{A}_A$ and $B_2, B_3 \in \mathfrak{A}_B$ be self-adjoint involutions ($A_1^2 = N^2 = B_2^2 = B_3^2 = \mathbf{1}$) with $[\mathfrak{A}_A, \mathfrak{A}_B] = \{0\}$, and let $f \in [0, 1]$ ($\ell = \sqrt{1 - f^2}$). Define the Semi-Brukner operator $K(f) = A_1(B_3 - B_2) - (N_+ + f N_-)(B_3 + B_2)$. Then in every state $\omega$ on $\mathfrak{A}$,
\begin{equation}
    |\omega(K(f))| \le B_{\max}^{\mathrm{SB}}(f) = 2\sqrt{1 + f^2} = 2\sqrt{2 - \ell^2}.
    \label{eq:theorem1_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From $A_1^2 = N^2 = \mathbf{1}$, direct expansion gives $N_\pm^* = N_\pm$, $A_1 N_+ = N_+ A_1$, $A_1 N_- = -N_- A_1$, $N_+ N_- + N_- N_+ = 0$, and $N_+^2 + N_-^2 = \mathbf{1}$. Setting $D_- = B_3 - B_2$ and $D_+ = B_3 + B_2$, the involutions $B_2^2 = B_3^2 = \mathbf{1}$ yield $D_\mp^* = D_\mp$, $D_-^2 + D_+^2 = 4\mathbf{1}$, and $D_- D_+ + D_+ D_- = 0$. Expanding $K(f)^2$ using $A_1 N_+ = N_+ A_1$, $A_1 N_- = -N_- A_1$, and $D_- D_+ + D_+ D_- = 0$ eliminates the $N_+$ cross-term and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K_squared}
\begin{aligned}
K(f)^2 &= D_-^2 + (N_+^2 + f^2 N_-^2) D_+^2 \\
       &\quad - f A_1 N_- (D_- D_+ - D_+ D_-).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Introduce the auxiliary elements $S_1 = f D_- + A_1 N_- D_+$ and $S_2 = f N_+ D_+$. Because $A_1$ and $N_-$ anticommute, $(A_1 N_-)^* = N_- A_1 = -A_1 N_-$ and $(A_1 N_-)^2 = -N_-^2$, whence $S_1^* = f D_- - A_1 N_- D_+$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S1S2_squares}
\begin{aligned}
S_1^* S_1 &= f^2 D_-^2 + N_-^2 D_+^2 + f A_1 N_- (D_- D_+ - D_+ D_-), \\
S_2^* S_2 &= f^2 N_+^2 D_+^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing Eqs.~\eqref{eq:K_squared} and~\eqref{eq:S1S2_squares} cancels the commutator term identically and yields the exact Sum-of-Squares identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sos_identity}
4(1 + f^2)\mathbf{1} - K(f)^2 = S_1^* S_1 + S_2^* S_2,
\end{equation}
since $N_+^2 + N_-^2 = \mathbf{1}$ and $D_-^2 + D_+^2 = 4\mathbf{1}$. For every state $\omega$ on $\mathfrak{A}$, positivity guarantees $\omega(S_1^* S_1) \ge 0$ and $\omega(S_2^* S_2) \ge 0$, so $|\omega(K(f))|^2 \le \omega(K(f)^2) \le 4(1 + f^2)$, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:theorem1_bound}. The bound is saturated on a two-qubit Bell state $|\Phi^+\rangle$ by choosing $A_1 = \sigma_z$, $N = \sigma_x$ ($N_+ = 0, N_- = \sigma_x$), and $B_{2,3} = \mp \cos\varphi\,\sigma_z - \sin\varphi\,\sigma_x$ with $\varphi = \arctan f$.
\end{proof}

Written in terms of the leakage, Eq.~\eqref{eq:theorem1_bound} reads $\bigl(\omega(K(f))/2\bigr)^2 + \ell^2 \le 2$: a complementarity relation between the attainable Semi-Brukner value and the which-way information held by the environment, in line with the duality reading of Eq.~\eqref{eq:polarity_convention}.

\subsection{Bilateral Two-Friend Dephasing and Quartic $\mathcal{O}(f^4)$ Suppression}

Theorem~1 reveals that in a single-friend setup ($f_A = f$, Bob without an uncomputed friend so $f_B = 1$), the violation excess above the LF bound $2$ behaves in the high-leakage limit ($f \to 0, \ell \to 1$) as
\begin{equation}
    \Delta S_{\mathrm{SB}}(f) = 2\sqrt{1 + f^2} - 2 = f^2 - \frac{1}{4}f^4 + \mathcal{O}(f^6).
    \label{eq:sb_quadratic}
\end{equation}
A qualitatively harsher scaling emerges in genuine two-friend scenarios where \emph{both} Charlie and Debbie establish local records ($f_A = f_B = f$); Proposition~\ref{prop:bilateral} below shows that the controlling quantity is the product $f_A f_B$. For qubit observables parameterized by Bloch unit vectors $\hat{u}_C, \hat{n}_x$ on $S_A$ and $\hat{u}_D, \hat{m}_y$ on $S_B$ ($x, y \in \{2, 3\}$), partial dephasing along the friends' axes maps the effective post-reversal Bloch vectors to
\begin{align}
    \vec{a}_x^{\mathrm{eff}}(f) &= f \hat{n}_x + (1 - f)(\hat{n}_x \cdot \hat{u}_C)\hat{u}_C, \nonumber \\
    \vec{b}_y^{\mathrm{eff}}(f) &= f \hat{m}_y + (1 - f)(\hat{m}_y \cdot \hat{u}_D)\hat{u}_D.
    \label{eq:bloch_dephase}
\end{align}

Evaluating $S_{\mathrm{Bruk}}$ on a maximally entangled state mixed with isotropic noise, $\rho_\mu = \mu |\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+| + (1 - \mu)\mathbb{I}_4/4$ with visibility $\mu \in (0, 1]$, rotational invariance allows fixing $\hat{u}_C = \hat{z}$, $\hat{n}_2 = (\sin\theta_A, 0, \cos\theta_A)$, and $\hat{u}_D(\delta) = (-\sin\delta, 0, -\cos\delta)$ in the X--Z plane (after mirror-reflecting Bob's $y$ coordinate). Maximizing analytically over $\hat{m}_2 \in S^2$ yields the exact reduction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:brukner_closed_form}
\begin{aligned}
B_{\max}^{\mathrm{Bruk}}(f, \mu) &= \mu \max_{\theta_A, \delta \in [0, \pi]} \Bigl\{
    \vec{v}_+(\theta_A) \cdot \hat{u}_D(\delta) \\
    &\quad + \bigl[ f^2 \|\vec{v}_-(\theta_A)\|^2 \\
    &\qquad + (1 - f^2)\bigl(\vec{v}_-(\theta_A) \cdot \hat{u}_D(\delta)\bigr)^2 \bigr]^{1/2} \Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\vec{v}_\pm(\theta_A) = (-f\sin\theta_A, 0, \mp 1 - \cos\theta_A)$. On the collinear friend branch ($\delta = 0$), Eq.~\eqref{eq:brukner_closed_form} reduces to $\mu\bigl[1 + c + \sqrt{(1-c)^2 + f^4(1-c^2)}\bigr]$ for $c = \cos\theta_A$, whose exact collinear maximum occurs at $c^* = 1/(1+f^2)$ and yields the analytical lower bound $\mu\bigl[2 + f^4/(1+f^2)\bigr]$. Because the $\delta$-gradient at $(\theta_A^*, \delta=0)$ equals $2f^4\sqrt{2+f^2}/(1+f^2)^2 > 0$, optimal friend axes tilt smoothly as $\delta^*(f) \simeq \sqrt{2}f^4$ for all $f > 0$, contributing only a $+\,2f^8$ correction at small $f$ (so $\Delta S_{\mathrm{Bruk}}(f) = f^4/(1+f^2) + \mathcal{O}(f^8)$ is asymptotically exact through $\mathcal{O}(f^6)$) before producing substantial tilted-axis gains at moderate-to-high coherence ($+8.3\%$ at $f=0.50$ and $+40.3\%$ at $f=0.80$).

Crucially, the exact same collinear quartic lower bound governs Bong \emph{et al.}'s genuine LF facet $S_{\mathrm{GLF}} \le 6$:

\begin{proposition}[Quartic Violation of Genuine LF]
At unit visibility ($\mu = 1$), the maximum quantum value $S_{\max}^{\mathrm{GLF}}(f)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ineq_glf} under symmetric coherence retention $f \in (0, 1]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    S_{\max}^{\mathrm{GLF}}(f) \ge 6 + \frac{f^4}{1 + f^2} = 6 + \frac{(1 - \ell^2)^2}{2 - \ell^2} > 6 \quad \forall f \in (0, 1].
    \label{eq:glf_quartic}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Prepare $|\Phi^+\rangle$ (so all single-party marginals vanish) and choose X--Z plane Bloch vectors $\hat{u}_C = \hat{u}_D = +\hat{z}$ ($A_1 = B_1 = \sigma_z$), $\hat{n}_2 = \hat{m}_2 = -\hat{z}$, and $\hat{n}_3 = (\sin\theta, 0, \cos\theta)$, $\hat{m}_3 = (-\sin\theta, 0, \cos\theta)$. Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:bloch_dephase}, $\vec{a}_2^{\mathrm{eff}} = \vec{b}_2^{\mathrm{eff}} = -\hat{z}$, $\vec{a}_3^{\mathrm{eff}} = (f\sin\theta, 0, \cos\theta)$, and $\vec{b}_3^{\mathrm{eff}} = (-f\sin\theta, 0, \cos\theta)$. Substituting the resulting correlators $E_{11} = E_{22} = +1$, $E_{12} = E_{21} = -1$, $E_{23} = E_{32} = -\cos\theta$, and $E_{33} = -f^2\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:ineq_glf} yields $S_{\mathrm{GLF}}(\theta; f) = 5 + 2\cos\theta + f^2\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta$. Setting $\cos\theta = 1/(1 + f^2)$ and $\sin\theta = f\sqrt{2 + f^2}/(1 + f^2)$ (which satisfies $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$, formally verified in \texttt{LFQuantumBounds.lean}) evaluates algebraically to $6 + f^4/(1 + f^2) > 6$ for every $f > 0$.
\end{proof}

The agreement between Eq.~\eqref{eq:glf_quartic} and the collinear Brukner bound is structural rather than a second independent result: on the strategy above $A_2 = -A_1$, $B_2 = -B_1$, and $E_{11} = 1$, so that $S_{\mathrm{GLF}} = 4 + (E_{11} + E_{13} + E_{31} - E_{33})$, a Brukner-class CHSH expression shifted by a constant. The globally optimized values do differ beyond leading order: numerically $\Delta S_{\mathrm{GLF}}(f) \approx f^4 - 0.6 f^6$, against $\Delta S_{\mathrm{Bruk}}(f) = f^4 - f^6 + \mathcal{O}(f^8)$.

For the CHSH-type functionals the quartic law can be made two-sided, and the bound shows that the quadratic-versus-quartic split counts leaky laboratories rather than distinguishing inequalities.

\begin{proposition}[Bilateral Dephasing Bound for CHSH-Type Functionals]
\label{prop:bilateral}
Let $\rho$ be an arbitrary two-qubit state, mixed with isotropic noise as $\mu\rho + (1 - \mu)\mathbb{I}_4/4$ with $\mu \in (0, 1]$, and let Charlie's and Debbie's records leak with coherence retentions $f_A, f_B \in [0, 1]$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bloch_dephase} with $f \to f_A$ in its first line and $f \to f_B$ in its second). Then each of $S_{\mathrm{Bruk}}$, $S_{\mathrm{BnLF}}$, and $S_{\mathrm{SB}}$ with Debbie present satisfies
\begin{equation}
    S \le 2\mu\sqrt{1 + f_A^2 f_B^2}.
    \label{eq:bilateral_bound}
\end{equation}
The bound is attained by $S_{\mathrm{BnLF}}$ and by the two-friend $S_{\mathrm{SB}}$ on $|\Phi^+\rangle$ with collinear friend axes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $D_A = f_A \mathbf{1} + (1 - f_A)\hat{u}_C \hat{u}_C^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $D_B = f_B \mathbf{1} + (1 - f_B)\hat{u}_D \hat{u}_D^{\mathsf{T}}$. By Eq.~\eqref{eq:bloch_dephase}, every setting entering the three functionals has an effective Bloch vector $D_A \hat{n}$ or $D_B \hat{m}$ with $\hat{n}, \hat{m}$ unit vectors; this includes the friends' own observables, since $D_A \hat{u}_C = \hat{u}_C$ and $D_B \hat{u}_D = \hat{u}_D$. Each functional is therefore a CHSH combination of bilinear forms $\hat{n} \cdot X \hat{m}$ in unit vectors, with $X = \mu D_A T D_B$ and $T_{ij} = \mathrm{Tr}[\rho\,\sigma_i \otimes \sigma_j]$ of operator norm $\|T\| \le 1$. The maximization over unit vectors behind the Horodecki criterion~\cite{horodecki1995}, which applies to any real $3 \times 3$ matrix, gives $S \le 2\sqrt{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2}$, where $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2$ are the two largest singular values of $X$. By Ky Fan's maximum principle, $\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 = \|X e_1\|^2 + \|X e_2\|^2$ for an orthonormal basis $\{e_1, e_2\}$ of a suitable two-dimensional subspace, and this basis can be chosen with $e_1 \perp \hat{u}_D$. Then $w_i = T D_B e_i$ obey $\|w_1\| \le f_B$ and $\|w_2\| \le 1$, and $D_A^2 = f_A^2 \mathbf{1} + (1 - f_A^2)\hat{u}_C \hat{u}_C^{\mathsf{T}}$ yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2}{\mu^2} &= f_A^2 \sum_{i} \|w_i\|^2 + (1 - f_A^2) \sum_{i} (\hat{u}_C \cdot w_i)^2 \\
    &\le f_A^2 (1 + f_B^2) + (1 - f_A^2) = 1 + f_A^2 f_B^2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
because $\sum_i (\hat{u}_C \cdot w_i)^2 = \sum_i (e_i \cdot D_B T^{\mathsf{T}} \hat{u}_C)^2 \le \|D_B T^{\mathsf{T}} \hat{u}_C\|^2 \le 1$. For equality, take $|\Phi^+\rangle$ and $\hat{u}_C = \hat{u}_D = \hat{z}$, so that $X = \mu\,\mathrm{diag}(F, -F, 1)$ with $F = f_A f_B$. Alice's raw directions $\hat{z}$ and $\hat{x}$, together with Bob's raw directions $(-F\hat{x} \pm \hat{z})/\sqrt{1 + F^2}$ assigned to his two settings according to the signs of the functional, give $S = 2\mu\sqrt{1 + F^2}$. These choices are admissible in $S_{\mathrm{BnLF}}$ and in the two-friend $S_{\mathrm{SB}}$, where only $A_1 = \sigma_z$ is pinned to a friend axis. In $S_{\mathrm{Bruk}}$ the requirement $B_1 = \hat{u}_D \cdot \vec{\sigma}$ excludes them, and numerically the Brukner optimum lies strictly below the bound for $0 < f < 1$ (Table~\ref{tab:scaling_comparison}).
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:bilateral} has three consequences. First, for $f_B = 1$ and $\mu = 1$ it reproduces the bound $2\sqrt{1 + f_A^2}$ of Theorem~1, which holds there without any restriction on dimension. Second, the quadratic law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sb_quadratic} characterizes the one-friend \emph{scenario}, not the Semi-Brukner functional: once Debbie is present and equally leaky, $B_2$ and $B_3$ are both dephased and $S_{\mathrm{SB}}$ saturates at $2\sqrt{1 + f^4}$, so its excess is quartic as well. For symmetric leakage the two-friend Brukner excess is correspondingly sandwiched,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{f^4}{1 + f^2} \le \Delta S_{\mathrm{Bruk}}(f) \le 2\sqrt{1 + f^4} - 2 \le f^4,
    \label{eq:brukner_sandwich}
\end{equation}
which makes quartic suppression a two-sided statement for two-qubit systems. In general the excess is governed by the product $f_A f_B$, and the one-friend and two-friend laws are its cases $f_B = 1$ and $f_B = f_A$. Third, at visibility $\mu$ the saturated functionals can exceed their bound $2$ if and only if $f_A f_B > \sqrt{\mu^{-2} - 1}$; for symmetric leakage the threshold is $f_{\mathrm{crit}} = (\mu^{-2} - 1)^{1/4}$, the square root of the one-friend threshold. Read the other way, a given leakage requires $\mu > (1 + f_A^2 f_B^2)^{-1/2}$: at $f = 0.2$ this is $\mu > 0.981$ with one leaky laboratory and $\mu > 0.9992$ with two, so the asymptotic regime $f \to 0$ is reachable only at visibilities very close to one.

Figure~\ref{fig:lf_leakage_bounds} and Table~\ref{tab:scaling_comparison} display the global numerical optimization across the five functionals alongside these analytical benchmarks. As seen in Fig.~\ref{fig:lf_leakage_bounds}(a) and~(b), when $98\%$ of record distinguishability leaks into unreversed modes ($\ell \approx 0.980, f = 0.20$), One-Friend Semi-Brukner retains a nearly $0.04$ violation excess, whereas Two-Friend Brukner and Genuine LF drop by a factor of $25$ to $+0.00154$--$0.00156$. Under realistic quantum-processor visibility $\mu < 1$, Semi-Brukner remains violable whenever $\mu > 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.7071$ above critical coherence $f_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{SB}}(\mu) = \sqrt{\mu^{-2} - 1}$ (tolerating leakage up to $\ell_{\max}^{\mathrm{SB}}(\mu) = \sqrt{2 - \mu^{-2}}$). With both laboratories equally leaky, Proposition~\ref{prop:bilateral} raises this to $f_{\mathrm{crit}} = (\mu^{-2} - 1)^{1/4}$ for the saturated CHSH-type functionals ($\approx 0.653$ at $\mu = 0.92$). Genuine LF requires substantially higher baseline fidelity and near-unit branch coherence ($f_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{GLF}} \approx 0.892$, $\ell_{\max}^{\mathrm{GLF}} \approx 0.453$ versus $f_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{SB}} \approx 0.426$, $\ell_{\max}^{\mathrm{SB}} \approx 0.905$ at $\mu = 0.92$, rising to $f_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{GLF}} \approx 0.937$ at $\mu = 0.88$, all for the Werner state $\rho_\mu$; admitting partially entangled states mixed with the same noise lowers the Genuine LF values only slightly, to about $0.88$ and $0.93$), opening a broad experimental window in which one-friend and Bell inequalities are violated while genuine two-friend LF facets remain strictly satisfied.
